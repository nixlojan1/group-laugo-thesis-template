This study aims to assess the effectiveness of sports complex facility management practices in terms of planning, maintenance, safety, scheduling, and user satisfaction. Specifically, it seeks to determine the current challenges encountered in managing sports facilities, evaluate the limitations of the existing system, and propose a technology-based facility management system that complies with recognized ISO software quality standards to improve operational efficiency, service quality, and sustainability of the sports complex.

In accordance with ISO/IEC 25010:2011 – Systems and Software Quality Requirements and Evaluation (SQuaRE), effective software systems should meet quality dimensions such as ‎functional suitability, usability, reliability, performance efficiency, and security. Traditional manual systems fail to meet these ISO quality standards due to their lack of automation, poor reliability, and limited accessibility. This study compares the following system variants.

  1. What problems are encountered in the existing sports complex facility management system in terms of scheduling, maintenance, safety, and monitoring?
  2. What ISO software quality dimensions are relevant in evaluating a proposed sports complex facility management system?
  3. How does the proposed system compare with the traditional system in terms of efficiency, accuracy, usability, and reliability?
